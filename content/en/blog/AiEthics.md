## Ethics in Data Science

If we reflect on this example, we find there are several guidelines that exist that users should meet to ensure ethical balance in the field of technology. We must first determine the difference between ethical concepts to recognize where a certain scenario falls short.  For instance, if we take a closer look into a court of law where a trial is taking place. The members of trial are all subject to fairness, firstly. This is to encourage procedural fairness in giving each party a voice during the trial. With only fairness however, certain cases can be swept under the rug. Additionally, certain members of the court can be influenced some way by an outside force. To combat these issues, a court must also include transparency for all people to achieve a just trial. Transparency is normally reached in the justice system by making the legal proceedings open and accessible to the public. Using this example of a trial in a court, we can extrapolate to our example in the previous section regarding the survey. The use of saved data by a potential third-party organization is not disclosed if one decides to fill in the survey. Furthermore, what said organization is doing with the data would likely not be known. They could sell this data to other organizations, or malicious actors in a worst-case scenario. 

This is a pressing issue in the big tech sector, where in 2018, Facebook and Meta were exposed for harvesting millions of users' data without their consent through a quiz, which was discovered by Cambridge Analytica in March of that year. This led to an outrage of users and a loss of 37 billion market value in Facebooks stock. If we analyze the fairness of this scenario, one can theoretically make a mental gymnastics argument in consent through signing up for the website itself. The problem with argument is that those who were taking the quiz did in fact provide consent. There were questions present on the quiz that received data for the friends of users, which resulted in the FTC penalizing Facebook for unclear and inaccurate consent methods.

## Needed Changes

If we are subject to these impediments of privacy, what can we really do about it? Now that we've discussed what can be problematic with the ever-present data mining from various technological sectors, we must try and decipher what people can do and are doing to prevent this. To begin we must define certain ethical concepts to understand what angle to approach it from. To achieve a certain level of privacy, data scientists can try and protect user data by classifying data into cohorts so to speak, this process is called k-anonymity. The basis of k-anonymity is that data must be generalized by replacing specific values with the following:

  - Generalized ranges of values such as 25-30 rather than 27.
  - Masking unique attribute combinations with "*".
  - Using appropriate k-value for the use case.

The usage of k-anonymity is expressed in several different areas, such as the healthcare sector, location-based services through masking techniques, and most census data in the world.

Another concept we must look at is the technique of overruling. What overruling does is deploy human intervention to reject or modify an LLM's final output if deemed harmful, inaccurate or unethical in nature. This process is typically mandatory in high-stake procedures such as trials or hiring. This is a great step to begin to improve not only LLMs but how we use them ethically.

Moving slightly towards encryption, the process of converting data from readable text through a key to an unreadable format called ciphertext. This concept has been in place for as long has humans have been around to cover information in confidentiality. The most obvious and widespread example of this is language, going as far back as ancient societies. In present-day however, encryption has become so complex in the way it is performed. Encrypting data in a modern sense has seen many different applications, in the intelligence field, a form of encryption called obfuscation is used by various agencies to hide or abstract vital information. 


This procedure is also done in technologies, for example in the manipulation of data, through misleading anonymity by grouping certain ranges to illustrate trends in healthcare. We should look at the famous case of Edward Snowden in 2013. Snowden used encryption methods to exfiltrate classified NSA documents such as the Bullrun program, an $800 million plan to crack modern encryption in its entirety. Snowden's efforts triggered a global adoption of encryption following the scandal. 

Through Snowden and others' efforts to promulgate many ethical foundations, we have made massive breakthroughs in the ethics of technology. However, as AI exponentially grows and adapts, methods of keeping privacy standards in has and will become harder to follow.
