## Ethics in Data Science

If we reflect on this example, we find there are several guidelines that exist that users should meet to ensure ethical balance in the field of technology. We must first determine the difference between ethical concepts to recognize where a certain scenario falls short.  For instance, if we take a closer look into a court of law where a trial is taking place. The members of trial are all subject to fairness, firstly. This is to encourage procedural fairness in giving each party a voice during the trial. With only fairness however, certain cases can be swept under the rug. Additionally, certain members of the court can be influenced some way by an outside force. To combat these issues, a court must also include transparency for all people to achieve a just trial. Transparency is  normally reached in the justice system by making the legal proceedings open and accessible to the public. Using this example of a trial in a court, we can extrapolate to our example in the previous section regarding the survey. The use of saved data by a potential third-party organization is not disclosed if one decides to fill in the survey. Furthermore, what said organization is doing with the data would likely not be known. They could sell this data to other organizations, or malicious actors in a worst-case scenario. This is a pressing issue in the big tech sector, where in 2018, Facebook and Meta was exposed for harvesting millions of users' data wihtout their consent through a quiz, which was discovered by Cambridge Analytica in March of that year. This led to an outrage of users and a loss of 37 billion market value in Facebooks stock. If we analyze the fairness of this scenario, one can theoretically make a mental-gymnastics arguement in consent through signing up for the website itself. The problem with with arguement is that those who were taking the quiz did in fact provide consent. There were questions present on the quiz that received data for the friends of users, which resulted in the FTC penalizing Facebook for unclear and inaccurate consent methods. 


## Why Should We Care?


If we do some reflecting on the aforementioned scenarios, one has to think: "if it's basically out of one's control, and we are financially neutral to this issue, why do we care?". The answer to this is quite simple, look back to future generations, as far back as you can think. The 1960s for example. This is an era of social and moral liberation. However in the 1940s-1950s particularily in the U.S, we see a time period very focused on the financial properity of the country rather than social issues at large. One could say things went backwards in this regard as in the first post-war period in the 1920s and 30s, the battle for womens rights made a great step forward for the country and world.  If we look now at Gen Z in comparison to other generations in the past like the Milennials, Gen Z as a whole is more progressive even if there were several movements such as the anti-war movement in the early 2000s as a result of the Bush administration. Gen Z typically wants authentiticty and transparency above all. These values stand in the midst of social outcast, financial burden, and  political issues. If we are strictly speaking about our most current adult generation there is nothing more important to them as holding our big market capital businesses accountable in their overarching reach in society.


 The most pressing aspect in their concern is the data marketplace, in which most of these companies are indirectly manipulating the user to leverage data in accordance with other connected organizations. They do this to improve their algorithm in general, but more specifically to alter users feeds potentially across several platforms. As a result, many users might notice that certain ads or suggestions might appear across all platforms, these of which were saved as data through many types of user input. However important this particular issue is, it has existed for a decade or so. We've been skeptical of the algorithm of certain platforms for quite a long time and have forcefully held these companies accountable for transparency in data, pharmacology, etc. Unfortunately, the rollout of Artificial Intelligence has left us with gargatuan task of trying to combat the almost invasive nature of how many LLMs neglect true privacy foundations. For instance, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said recently that if a court issues a subpoena, OpenAI has to provide often times user-sensitive data as it pertains to a case. The other side of the coin is that ChatGPT is not accountable for any information it provides however harmful, incorrect, or incriminating. This means that if Gen Z is adamant on keeping authenticity and transparency universal, then we must implement usage and data laws against these platforms. Some areas are doing more than others currently as in 2024, the European Union passed a Artifical Intelligence act where high-risk AI models are not-exempt from punishment and their outputs are held accountable. Although promising, the caveat is that many large corporations can capitalize on legal loopholes in the act and effectively dampen the regulations presented by it. Generally, we as a society should be aware of the personal data implications and strive to limit this technology to it's supposed initial purpose.
