## Our Current Global State & History


{{< figure src="/images/blog/windows95.jpg" alt="windows" width="600" caption="Launch day of Microsoft Windows 95" >}}

Think of where the world sits currently, Big Data surging, incredible medical breakthroughs every year, continuation of social and geopolitical justice. Now, in 2026, we have supposed access to instant information at our fingertips whenever and wherever we want, in a personalized configuration through the implementation of Artificial Intelligence. Through endless training and data mining AI is experiencing an exponential trend of explosive advancement with the world struggling to keep up with it, scrambling to understand the moral and ethical implications of this ever-growing technology. Let's scale this entire conversation back a bit, on July 14, 1995, Microsoft released the first consumer-oriented operating system, without the dependent foundation of the MS-DOS framework, Windows 95. This completely broke down the wall of technological possibility, as with previous installations of Windows the user was presented with a graphical shell essentially layered over the MS-DOS operating system, omitting Windows NT. The release of Windows 95 was a major catalyst in the progression of the internet also, as whilst it did not allow for TCP/IP at its launch it did provide key trailblazing network features such as DHCPP and dial-up networking through then popular services like AOL.

If we take into consideration the presence of accelerating change in respect to technology, it is our responsibility as humans to adapt consequentially to our own creations, and mandatory when our technological advancements create divide or confusion in other parts of our lives. I think it is worthwhile to ponder how different areas of our world adjust to society's lightspeed developments, as in many cases it can place a country within an unfavorable situation. For example, in May 2021, a ransomware group called DarkSide gained access to Colonial Pipeline, the largest products pipeline in the entire United States which transports over 100 million gallons of fuel every day across the entire East Coast. The attack was so severe that it resulted in the Biden Administration declaring a state of emergency. DarkSide accessed the network with relative ease as the system did not have multifactor identification protocols in place. As a result, the pipeline shutdown spanned over a 5-day period between May 7-12 of 2021. The ransom of the access was set at 75 bitcoin, approximately worth $4.4 million. The Department of Justice recovered 3/4ths of the ransom, but the company experienced severe reputational decline as it is highly discouraged by the FBI to provide such payments to these types of organizations.

Shortly after the attack, various plaintiffs in the class action lawsuit sued Colonial Pipeline for negligence, in which the statement assessed that the incident was responsible for the negative impact towards 11,000 fuel retailers across the country. While the suit was unsuccessful, the cost expenditure was monumental for the company, and during the brutal aftermath the US Department of Transportation issued a compliance order which mandated Colonial Pipeline a civil penalty of $1 million USD.


## Societal Necessity

{{< figure src="/images/blog/qiang.jpg" alt="China" width="600" caption="Current Chinese Premier Li Qiang" >}}

The way technology has polarized the globe over the past 20 years is remarkable, with a portion of the world population believing that technology should be strictly controlled under ethical laws pertaining to personal data and should have no place in authoritative roles. The way that technology grows is not only global however, as one can make the case that modern technology must also fit the mould of the system it occupies. For instance, In places like China there exists a semi-reliant nature for Artificial Intelligence to operate within China's titanic infrastructure. (“China’s “AI+” Drive Aims for Integration across Sectors: A Wake-up Call for Europe”) This reliance manifests as Industrial and Government Integration, as China is pushing towards a infusion of AI into core sectors including manufacturing, finance, logistics and public administration. Arguably most interesting however, is the full-fledged use of AI in the healthcare sector.(Riehl) Specifically, the Zhongshan Hospital in Shanghai is developing an AI programmed to act as a pilot which aggregates medical and personal information to assess and recommend certain healthcare and lifestyle interventions to individuals, and even more entire hospitals across China. Additionally, the growth and importance of AI in China allows them to create distinct AI-driven products and services which other regions are yet to explore. In fact, on July 26th, 2025, Chinese Premier Li Qiang unveiled China's comprehensive 13-point plan of Action for AI Governance at the World Artificial Intelligence Conference. (“South China Morning Post”)

The Plan of Action contained aspects concerning the national relationship with AI, as Li Qiang described the rapid development and monetization of AI as an "exclusive game" in which only a few players will have a stake in the sector. Within the plans' governance framework, Open-source development is a major point of focus as the plan advocates for building reliable open-source platforms to lower the floor for technological innovation and application. In turn, enhancing the inclusiveness of AI services across the country. Outlined across a ten year timespan, this cohesive plan hopes to implement sustainable AI initaitves such as energy-efficient chips, low power computing and precise benchmarks to reduce AI's carbon footprint. As a result of the plan, China aims to push for full-integration in their economy by 2035. The way that China views the development and popularization of AI is seen more positively is potentially due to the structure of China's society. For instance, China is very keen on improving their infrastructure as a whole population rather than implementing codes towards individual privacy, as although they set comprehensive data protection laws in 2021 the Chinese government still has the authority to access personal information against personal privacy. 


## The Western Perspective

{{< figure src="/images/blog/unitedNations.jpg" alt="UN" width="600" caption="U.N Security Council Meeting, 2023" >}}

Contrasting the state-driven AI machine that China operates, the UN and European Union approach the situation in a more holsitic manner. If we look at China's usage of Artificial Intelligence within it's polarizing social credit systems, one can make the arguement that AI used in this fashion is the anithesis of respecting human rights and privacy. To combat the potential exploitation of AI, the European Union, similarly to China, looks to ensure that open-source platforms are readily available to the public. Unlike China however, in 2024 the EU implemented the 2024 EU Act. The act operates as the world's first comprehensive AI Law, with heavy scaling towards ethics and risk-containment as it pertains to the expansion of Artifical Intelligence technology. If we analyze the laws' risk-containment framework we see that the unfavorable ethical outcomes of AI can be limited to a degree. For instance, the law outlines that systems that pose a visable and noticable threat to human rights such as social scoring and using biometrics identification in public settings must be banned across the European Union. If we go down the risk ladder, the framework claims that AI systems that work amongst high but not unacceptable risk sectors such as healthcare and law enforcement must comply with extremely strict guidelines that prevent AI from taking authoritative actions. Despite the several positive influences from the Law, there are still points of contention within it. For example, the lower risk systems are susceptible to bias as the use and distribution of deepfakes and synthetic information is classified as minimal-risk systems. In general, The EU is more concerned with it's ability to act the leader in the ethical application of Artifical Intelligence, opting for privacy and maintenence of human rights rather than large-scale infrastructure developement at a cost. (European Commission)






## Where it Leaves Us

{{< figure src="/images/blog/meeting.jpg" alt="meeting" width="600" caption="U.N Security Council Meeting, 2023" >}}

As AI builds it's monumental capacity, it is for us as a human society to realize that not every country can benefit from the same solution. When a country that prioritizes economic and technological growth first, one cannot expect that a technology with unlimited financial potential would be severely limited in this system. On the other side of the coin, countries that strive for ethical and social progression will not thrive under the monetary ideals that China appreciates at it concerns AI.  
The important thing that most countries have recognized is that AI has the potential to deviate to unfathomable outcomes, which can prove detrimental to society as a whole. Take for example blue washing at events or summits, where companies/organizations display their ethical activism but continue selling personal user data to authority/third party organizations contradicting their position. Similarly green washing parties promote themselves as environmentally sound when they exploit these same issues behind the scenes for monetary gain. These unethical practices normalize the exploitation of services which justifies the means to an end.
